# Guillotine Format Strategy

Yahoo labels this league's scoring type "Death." Combined with the waiver-rule reference to "players dropped due to guillotine elimination," this is a **guillotine (elimination) league**: rather than a normal season culminating in fixed playoffs, some number of teams get eliminated over the course of the season based on weekly scoring, and their rosters are released back to free agency/waivers (fast 1-day clearing, vs. the normal 2-day period).

## What this changes about roster construction

- **Floor > ceiling.** A player who can put up a zero (bye-prone role, injury-prone, boom/bust volatility) is a bigger liability than in a normal season-long league, because one bad week can end your season outright regardless of your roster's talent the rest of the way. Prioritize weekly-safe floors, especially at positions/slots where you have no depth behind them.
- **Depth matters more than star power alone.** Stars-and-scrubs auction strategies (spend 70%+ of budget on 2-3 elite players, fill the rest with $1 fliers) are riskier here — a thin bench can't absorb a single bad week from a studded-up starting lineup. Lean more balanced than you would in a standard ESPN-style season-long league.
- **Bye weeks are a real elimination risk, not just an inconvenience.** Stacking multiple starters' byes in the same week is dangerous in a format where a bad single week can knock you out. Check bye-week clustering more aggressively than usual.
- **Watch the standings, not just your own roster.** Your elimination risk each week is relative to the other 17 teams, not an absolute bar — a "good" score can still be last place in a high-scoring week. Track league-wide scoring trends if visible, not just your own team's projections.

## In-season / waiver implications

- Every elimination dumps a full 14-man roster (minus IR-locked pieces) back onto waivers on a fast 1-day clock. This means **real talent will periodically flood the waiver wire mid-season** — often better than what's normally available in a non-guillotine league at that point in the season.
- Keep FAAB in reserve rather than spending it all early — a post-elimination talent drop can be worth more than a speculative early-season stash.
- No trades are allowed all season, so waiver claims are the *only* lever to react to a bad week, an injury, or an elimination-driven talent drop. Be decisive and don't assume you can trade your way out of a mistake later.
